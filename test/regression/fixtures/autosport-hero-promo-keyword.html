Why Gasly's Monaco GP penalty saga risks a regulatory labyrinth

The stewards' handling of Pierre Gasly's penalty has raised questions that go far beyond a single race, touching on how the regulations are meant to be applied consistently across a season.

At the heart of the matter is whether a precedent set in Monaco can fairly be carried over to circuits with entirely different characteristics, and what that means for drivers caught in similar situations later in the year.

If the answer is that each case must be judged on its own merits, the sport risks a regulatory labyrinth in which no two decisions look alike and fairness becomes impossible to guarantee.
