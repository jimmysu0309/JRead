March 2008, rev. June 2008

Technology tends to separate normal from natural. Our bodies weren't designed to eat the foods that people in rich countries eat, or to get so little exercise. There may be a similar problem with the way we work: a normal job may be as bad for us intellectually as white flour or sugar is for us physically.

I began to suspect this after spending several years working with startup founders. I've now worked with over 200 of them, and I've noticed a definite difference between programmers working on their own startups and those working for big companies. I wouldn't say founders seem happier, necessarily; starting a startup can be very stressful. But they seem more able to focus, and to be able to work on what genuinely interests them.

What's so unnatural about working for a big company? The root of the problem is that humans weren't meant to work in such large groups. See footnote [1] for the details.
