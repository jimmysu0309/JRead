Our long national sunscreen nightmare is almost over

On TikTok, the tanned youths are explaining why they no longer wear sunscreen, describing how you can naturally build up a solar callus or sun tolerance to getting burned, which is of course completely false and dangerous advice.

For the past 26 years, American sunscreens have been stuck with an outdated roster of chemical filters because the FDA regulates sunscreen as an over-the-counter drug, a far higher bar than the cosmetics rules used across Europe and Asia.

That has consequences. Teens are purportedly flocking back to tanning beds, and the broader anti-sunscreen movement keeps gaining traction online even though the underlying claims have no scientific support whatsoever.

The new filter is bemotrizinol, also known as Tinosorb S or BEMT, and the reason it is such a big deal is that it offers broad-spectrum protection against both UVA and UVB rays while being far more photostable than older filters.

Another side effect of the FDA's laborious approvals process is that it creates a gray market for the good stuff, with people badgering friends traveling abroad to bring back bottles of Korean and Japanese sunscreen.
