May. 24, 2026 By Marc Santia and Carol Leonnig

A federal grand jury in Washington, D.C., returned a multi-count indictment Thursday against the man accused of opening fire near the White House security checkpoint last month, according to court documents unsealed Friday morning. The indictment charges the suspect with assault on a federal officer, possession of a firearm in furtherance of a crime of violence, and carrying a firearm without a license.

Prosecutors said the suspect approached the South Lawn checkpoint at approximately 8:30 p.m. on April 15 and opened fire on uniformed Secret Service officers stationed at the post. Two agents returned fire, striking the suspect multiple times. He was transported to George Washington University Hospital, where he remained in critical condition for several days before being transferred to a federal detention facility.

The investigation, led by the FBI and the U.S. Secret Service, included a review of surveillance footage from multiple cameras covering the checkpoint area. Witnesses reported hearing six to eight gunshots in rapid succession before the officers neutralized the threat. A bystander who was grazed by a ricochet was treated and released from a nearby hospital.
