Remembering David Hockney (1937–2026)

David Hockney, who has died at the age of 88, was one of the most influential and beloved British artists of the 20th and 21st centuries, a painter whose joyful palette and restless curiosity reshaped how a generation saw colour, light and the everyday world around them.

Born in Bradford in 1937, Hockney rose to prominence in the 1960s with a body of work that fused pop sensibility with a deeply personal vision, from the sun-drenched swimming pools of Los Angeles to the rolling landscapes of his native Yorkshire.

Hockney never lost his ravenous appetite for new tools and techniques, embracing the fax machine, the photocopier and, in his later years, the iPad, which he used to capture the changing seasons outside his one-time studio in Normandy with the same delight he once brought to oil and acrylic.

He is survived by a body of work that spans more than six decades and a public that adored him not just for the pictures but for the irrepressible curiosity and joy with which he made them, right up until the very end of his long and extraordinary life.
