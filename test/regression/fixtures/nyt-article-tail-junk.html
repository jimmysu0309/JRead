Stephen Colbert’s Last ‘Late Show’: Laughing Well Is the Best Revenge

He didn’t land the pope, but he got a Beatle. The final broadcast was a sprawling celebration of a decade of late-night television, packed with cameos and callbacks that rewarded longtime viewers of the program with a parade of familiar faces.

The host opened the evening with a monologue that looked back at the show’s beginnings, joking that the network had given him just enough rope over the years and that he had decided to use it to tie a very elegant bow on the whole affair.

Musical guests joined for an extended singalong that closed the broadcast, and the studio audience stayed on its feet through most of the final segment, a send-off that felt less like a funeral than a graduation ceremony for everyone involved.

In a discussion of the legacy media landscape, the essay argued that a version of the institution will survive in streaming clips and podcasts, but the communal ritual of watching the same desk at the same hour appears in print on its way out, a phrase critics repeated all week in tributes that ran far longer than this sentence ever could.

James Poniewozik is the chief TV critic for The Times. He writes reviews and essays with an emphasis on television as it reflects a changing culture and politics, and has covered the beat for more than two decades.
