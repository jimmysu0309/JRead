Why Is Everything Spicy Now?

More Americans are setting their mouth on fire—for extreme sport, and for everyday thrills.

By Ellen Cushing

August 18, 2025

A decade ago, ordering the spiciest thing on the menu was a niche pursuit, the province of chile-heads and masochists trading tips on obscure internet forums late into the night.

Today it is a mainstream American pastime, and the appetite shows no sign of cooling as hot sauce sales climb year after year across every region of the country.

Researchers who study why people willingly subject themselves to pain call it benign masochism: the pleasure of a threat your body registers as dangerous but your mind knows is safe.

Whatever the cause, the trend has reshaped grocery aisles, restaurant menus, and the way a whole generation of eaters thinks about flavor, thrill, and a mouth that will not stop burning.
