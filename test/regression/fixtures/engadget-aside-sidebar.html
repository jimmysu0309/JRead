Dyson PencilVac Fluffycones review — almost the perfect floor cleaner for tiny apartments

ENGADGET_MAIN_MARK The Dyson PencilVac is the most minimalist stick vacuum I've ever used. At just 1.8kg it weighs less than a bag of rice and the pencil-thin design means you can slip it into tight gaps between the fridge and the kitchen cabinet without breaking a sweat. The battery life is a solid forty minutes on the lowest setting and around fifteen minutes in the highest boost mode. Charging takes roughly three and a half hours from empty to full via the included dock which lives on the wall.

Using the PencilVac feels remarkably different from Dyson's heavier V15 and Gen5 models. Because it's light and the motor sits inside the pencil tube rather than at the handle the balance feels front-forward which takes maybe a day to get used to. Once you adjust you notice that the vacuum glides across hardwood floors and low-pile rugs without having to fight it. The twin Fluffycones roller heads do a good job of picking up long hair and pet fur without tangling which has been the achilles heel of every stick vacuum I've previously tested.

Noise is another area where the PencilVac outperforms its siblings. On the lowest setting you can carry a conversation without raising your voice. On boost mode it's audible but still noticeably quieter than the V15 Detect which sounds like a small hairdryer when running at full power. The dust bin is small at 0.1 litres which means emptying it after every two or three cleaning sessions in a one bedroom apartment but the one-click dust release makes the chore as painless as possible.

Suction performance is the PencilVac's weakest area compared to Dyson's larger vacuums. On carpets with deeply embedded dust you'll need to run boost mode to get acceptable pickup and the battery drain is steep at that setting. For apartments with mostly hardwood or tile and only the occasional rug the standard setting handles everything without complaint.

Image by Mat Smith for Engadget

The PencilVac also comes with that Dyson premium too priced at 600 dollars. That's still less than the company's other recent stick vacuums. For example that's 50 dollars less than the aforementioned V15 Detect. If your home has a lot of carpeting or rugs to clean the bigger more powerful models might be a better choice. The newer PencilVac Fluffy has a more traditional dual-roller system and it's also 150 dollars cheaper.

With its minimalist form factor the PencilVac is still an engineering marvel. Its high degree of mobility makes it easy to clean in tight corners and between furniture. I just wish it were slightly more powerful.
